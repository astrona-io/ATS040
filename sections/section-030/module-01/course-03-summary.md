# Summary

Governance describes how a project decides things. A foundation such as the CNCF supports projects with neutral stewardship and shared infrastructure, and each project's own maintainers and published documents decide everyday technical questions. The CNCF levels Sandbox, Incubating and Graduated describe how far a project has progressed through the foundation's review of adoption, governance and practice. They are not a quality ranking and not a security guarantee, and the status of a project can change, so check it when a decision depends on it.

Licenses and contribution rules answer different questions. A license states what you may do with the software and under which conditions. A contribution process states what a project needs from a change before it accepts it, such as a Developer Certificate of Origin sign-off, tests and a code of conduct. Meeting one does not satisfy the other. A dependency decision looks at the license, the intended use and evidence of upkeep, and it records the result.

A contribution starts with a focused issue that states the problem, the expected behavior and the evidence. A pull request proposes a change for review, and review is a conversation that can end in a merge, a request for changes or a decision not to proceed. Special Interest Groups own ongoing areas of Kubernetes, and working groups coordinate bounded efforts that cross several areas. The scope and charter of a group show where a proposal belongs.

On a cluster, the same habits apply to every change. Describe the problem, make the smallest correct change, review the difference before applying it and record why the change was made.

- A maturity level is one input to a choice, not the choice.
- Open source does not mean free of conditions.
- Opening a request starts a review. It does not promise a merge.
- Choose a group from its published scope.
